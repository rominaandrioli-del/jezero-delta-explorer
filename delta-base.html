Mission Brief

You have arrived at the base of Jezero Crater's ancient river delta. Billions of years ago, water flowed into this crater and deposited sediments that gradually formed the fan-shaped structure visible today. Scientists believe this environment may have been capable of supporting microbial life.

What You See

From your position at the foot of the delta, you can observe layered deposits that were carried by flowing water long ago. Each layer represents a chapter in the environmental history of Mars, recording changes in water flow, sediment transport, and climate over time.

Why It Matters

Deltas are excellent locations for preserving evidence of past environments. On Earth, river deltas often trap and preserve organic material and chemical signatures from surrounding regions. Studying similar deposits on Mars may help scientists understand whether the planet once offered conditions suitable for life.

Astronaut Decision

You have limited EVA time and energy reserves. Choose your next action: Explore nearby sediment layers in greater detail or continue toward the top of the delta to gain a broader view of the exploration region.

Mission Note

The delta is one of the primary reasons Jezero Crater was selected as the landing site for the Perseverance mission. The deposits found here are among the most promising locations on Mars for investigating the planet's ancient environmental history.
