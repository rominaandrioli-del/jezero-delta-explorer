Mission Brief

Welcome to Jezero Crater, the landing site of NASA's Perseverance rover. This crater once hosted a lake fed by an ancient river system. Your mission is to investigate locations that may preserve evidence of Mars' past habitability.

What You See

The landscape is mostly flat, with scattered rocks and low ridges extending toward the horizon. Although it appears dry and barren today, this region was shaped billions of years ago by flowing water and geological processes.

Why It Matters

This landing area provides access to several scientifically important targets, including the ancient delta deposits and sedimentary rock formations that may retain information about Mars' environmental history.

Mission Note

The Octavia E. Butler Landing Site was selected because it provides safe access to some of the most scientifically valuable terrain within Jezero Crater. From this starting point, explorers can reach the ancient river delta, sedimentary rock outcrops, and future sampling locations that may help reveal whether Mars once hosted habitable environments.
