Mission Brief

You have reached the top of the ancient delta. From this elevated position, you can observe a broader section of Jezero Crater and identify potential science targets for the next stage of the mission. The ancient river system that once fed the crater deposited sediments here over long periods of time, creating a valuable geological record of Mars' past.

What You See

From this vantage point, the landscape reveals the scale of the ancient environment. The delta extends across the crater floor, while distant ridges and rock formations mark locations that may contain clues about Mars' geological and climatic history.

Why It Matters

A high observation point allows explorers to make informed decisions before committing valuable EVA time and resources. Just as field geologists on Earth use elevated viewpoints to plan their surveys, astronauts can use this location to identify promising sampling sites, estimate travel distances, and avoid potentially hazardous terrain.

Astronaut Decision

From your elevated position, you must choose the mission's next priority. Travel toward Enchanted Lake to investigate layered sedimentary rocks, or head directly toward a sampling location to maximize collection opportunities. Consider the trade-off between gathering additional observations and conserving resources for sample acquisition.

Mission Note

Exploration missions depend on balancing scientific ambition with operational safety. A well-chosen route can reveal new discoveries while ensuring the crew remains within mission constraints. From Delta Top, astronauts can evaluate the terrain ahead and select the path that offers the greatest scientific return.
