Mission Brief

You have arrived at Wildcat Ridge, one of the most scientifically significant locations explored by the Perseverance rover. Samples collected from this area were considered valuable enough to be stored for potential return to Earth in a future mission. The sedimentary rocks exposed here formed within the ancient delta environment and may preserve evidence of the conditions that existed when water was present in Jezero Crater.

What You See

The outcrop before you contains finely layered sedimentary rocks deposited billions of years ago as river-borne sediments accumulated within an ancient lake. Differences in texture, color, and layering reveal changes in the environment over time. These rocks represent one of the clearest records of Jezero Crater's watery past.

Why It Matters

Not every rock is worth sampling. A Mars astronaut must carefully balance scientific value against limited EVA time, energy, and sample storage capacity. Wildcat Ridge stands out as a particularly promising target in the search for evidence of ancient habitable environments.

Astronaut Decision

Your sample container capacity is limited. Will you collect a core sample from Wildcat Ridge and allocate part of the mission's remaining sample capacity to this site, or continue searching for another target that might provide even greater scientific value? Consider the trade-off between securing a valuable sample now and preserving resources for future discoveries.

Hazard Assessment

  • Terrain Difficulty: Low
  • Visibility: Excellent
  • Navigation Risk: Low
  • Science Value: Extremely High
  • Sample Priority: Critical
  • EVA Priority: Sample Collection

Mission Note

Future exploration missions depend on identifying and preserving the most scientifically valuable samples. Wildcat Ridge demonstrates how astronauts must combine field observations, geological reasoning, and mission planning to decide which materials deserve detailed study and possible return to Earth.
