Mission Brief

You have arrived at Enchanted Lake, a rocky outcrop located near the base of Jezero Crater's ancient delta. This site contains layered sedimentary rocks that were deposited when water flowed through the crater billions of years ago. Scientists regard these deposits as some of the most promising targets for understanding Mars' past environment and its potential to support life.

What You See

The exposed rock layers in front of you tell a story written over millions of years. Unlike volcanic rocks, which form from cooling magma, these sediments were transported and deposited by water. Their layered structure preserves a record of changing environmental conditions, much like pages in a history book.

Why It Matters

On Earth, sedimentary rocks are excellent archives of environmental history and can preserve traces of past biological activity. For this reason, the Perseverance science team considered sites like Enchanted Lake high-priority exploration targets. By studying these rocks, future astronauts may uncover evidence of ancient habitable environments and identify the most valuable samples for return to Earth.

Astronaut Decision

Detailed analysis of this outcrop will require additional EVA time. Will you conduct a close inspection of the layered sediments and search for sampling targets, or continue toward Wildcat Ridge to reach a previously identified high-priority collection site?

Mission Note

The first close-up views of the Enchanted Lake outcrop generated considerable excitement because they provided a detailed look at sedimentary rocks within Jezero's delta system. These formations represent some of the strongest evidence that water once transported and deposited material in this region of Mars.
