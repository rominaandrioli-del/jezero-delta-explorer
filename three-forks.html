Mission Brief

You have reached Three Forks Sample Depot, a location selected as a backup cache site for scientifically valuable Martian samples. This site represents the final stage of the exploration workflow: preserving carefully selected specimens so they can be recovered and analyzed by future missions.

What You See

The landscape appears quiet and unremarkable, but its importance lies in what could be left behind. This area serves as a strategic storage location where samples can remain safely accessible for future retrieval missions.

Why It Matters

Future Mars exploration will rely on carefully documented and preserved samples. A single rock core may contain information about volcanic activity, the history of water, or the environmental conditions that existed billions of years ago. By establishing a secure depot, explorers create a bridge between fieldwork on Mars and advanced laboratory analysis on Earth.

Astronaut Decision

Mission resources are running low, and it is time to prioritize long-term scientific return. Will you deposit the collected sample for future recovery, ensuring it remains available to later missions, or retain it while continuing to search for additional science targets? Consider the balance between immediate exploration goals and the long-term success of sample return efforts.

Hazard Assessment

  • Science Value: Medium
  • Sample Priority: Mission Critical
  • EVA Priority: Sample Preservation

Mission Note

Exploration is a collaborative effort that extends across decades. The samples secured at locations such as Three Forks may eventually help answer some of the most important questions in planetary science: How did Mars evolve? Did it once host habitable environments? And could evidence of ancient life still be preserved within its rocks?
